Sun

Our Sun is a yellow dwarf star, a hot ball of glowing gases at the heart of our solar system. Its gravity holds the solar system together, keeping everything – from the biggest planets to the smallest particles of debris – in its orbit. Though it is special to us, there are billions of stars like our Sun scattered across the Milky Way galaxy.

Structure:

The Sun, like others stars, is a ball of gas. In terms of the number of atoms, it is made of 91.0% hydrogen and 8.9% helium. By mass, the Sun is about 70.6% hydrogen and 27.4% helium. The Sun has six regions: The core, the radiative zone, and the convective zone in the interior; the visible surface, called the photosphere; the chromosphere; and the outermost region, the corona.

Orbit:

Sun to have orbit like every other planet. But not around some other star like our sun but around a Super Massive Black Hole(Saggitarus A*) which is in the center of our Galaxy. From there, the Sun orbits the center of the Milky Way galaxy, bringing the planets, asteroids, comets, and other objects along with it. Our solar system is moving at an average velocity of 450,000 miles per hour (720,000 kilometers per hour). But even at this speed, it takes us about 230 million years to make one complete orbit around the Milky Way.

Moons:

The Sun and other stars don't have moons; instead, they have planets and their moons, along with asteroids, comets, and other objects.

Facts:

1. The Sun has many names in many cultures. The Latin word for Sun is “sol,” which is the main adjective for all things Sun-related: solar.

2. The Sun is the largest object in our solar system, comprising 99.8% of the system’s mass. Though it seems huge to us, the Sun isn't as large as other types of stars

3. Without the Sun's intense energy, there would be no life on Earth.

4. Different parts of the Sun rotate at different speeds.

5. One day the Sun will consume the Earth.
