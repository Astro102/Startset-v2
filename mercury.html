Mercury

Mercury is the innermost planet of the Solar System. Because it has almost no atmosphere to retain heat. Mercury's surface experience the greatest temperature variation among all other planets, which is 473°C during day time and -183°C during night and surface temperarure of 163°C. MErcury has weeker gravity as compared to Earth for obvious reasosn as Mercury is samll in size which is 3.7 m/s2 (38% of Earth's Gravity, 0.38g).

Structure:

The crust is similar in appearance to the that of the Moon with mare-like plains and heavy cratering. A distinctive feature of the surface crust is the presence of numerous narrow ridges, which might have formed as Mercury's core and mantle cooled and contracted after the crust has solified.

CORE: Mercury has a core of high rich molten iron which directly affects the density of the planet plus mercury has the highest content of iron as compared to all planets in the solar system.

Orbit:

Mercury speeds around the sun every 88 Earth days, traveling through space at nearly 112,000 mph (180,000 km/h), faster than any other planet. Its oval-shaped orbit is highly elliptical, taking Mercury as close as 29 million miles (47 million km) and as far as 43 million miles (70 million km) from the sun. If one could stand on Mercury when it is nearest to the sun, it would appear more than three times as large as it does when viewed from Earth.

Craters:

Mercury is the smallest planet — it is only slightly larger than Earth's moon. Since it has no significant atmosphere to stop impacts, the planet is pockmarked with craters. About 4 billion years ago, an asteroid roughly 60 miles (100 km) wide struck Mercury with an impact equal to 1 trillion 1-megaton bombs, creating a vast impact crater roughly 960 miles (1,550 km) wide. Known as the Caloris Basin, this crater could hold the entire state of Gujrat. Another large impact may have helped create the planet's odd spin.

Facts to know:

1. Mercury is so close to the sun, it is hard to directly observe it from Earth except during dawn or twilight.

2. It is named after the ancient Roman god of trade and profit.

3. There is no water on Mercury. But there might be a little ice in deep craters.

4. The core of mercury consists mainly of iron, like the Earth’s one, and it accounts for about 60% of the total planetary mass.

5. There are clouds at its poles sometimes. This phenomenon has not yet been studied.
