Galaxies to know

1.Andromeda Galaxy:

Andromeda, also known as M31, is the nearest major galaxy to our own Milky Way at a distance of 2.5 million light-years, making it an ideal natural laboratory to study star formation and galaxy evolution. The Andromeda Galaxy has a diameter of about 220,000 lightyears, making it the largest member of the Local Group in terms of extension.The Milky Way and Andromeda galaxies are expected to collide in around 4-5 billion years, merging to form a giant elliptical galaxy. The Andromeda Galaxy is among the brightest of the Messier objects, making it visible to the naked eye from Earth on moonless nights, even when viewed from areas with moderate light pollution.

2.Black Eye Galaxy:

The Black Eye Galaxy is a relatively isolated spiral galaxy 17 million light-years away in the mildly northern constellation of Coma Berenices. It was discovered by Edward Pigott in March 1779. A dark band of absorbing dust partially in front of its bright nucleus gave rise to its nicknames of the "Black Eye", "Evil Eye", or "Sleeping Beauty" galaxy. Black Eye is well known among amateur astronomers due to its form in small telescopes and visibility across inhabited latitudes. The interstellar medium of Messier 64 consists of two counter-rotating disks that are approximately equal in mass. The inner disk contains the prominent dust lanes of the galaxy.

3.Cigar Galaxy:

Cigar Galaxy is a starburst galaxy approximately 12 million light-years away in the constellation Ursa Major. A member of the M81(Messier 81) Group, it is about five times more luminous than the Milky Way and has a center one hundred times more luminous. Cigar Galaxy was believed to be an irregular galaxy. However, two symmetric spiral arms were discovered in near-infrared (NIR) images of the galaxy. It was discovered by Johann Elert Bode in 1774, he described it as a "nebulous patch", "very pale and of elongated shape".
